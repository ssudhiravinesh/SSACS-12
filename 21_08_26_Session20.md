## 1. What is a Computer Network?

A **computer network** is a collection of interconnected computers and peripheral devices (like printers or scanners) that can communicate with each other and share resources.

### Primary Objectives of Networking:
* **Resource Sharing:** Sharing physical hardware (like a single printer for an office) or software programs and files.
* **Communication:** Facilitating quick message and file exchanges (e.g., emails, chats).
* **Reliability:** Data can be backed up on multiple machines. If one fails, others have copies.
* **Cost Reduction:** Sharing resources prevents having to purchase expensive equipment for every individual user.

## 2. Evolution of Networking (The Milestones)

The modern Internet did not appear overnight. It evolved through three main stages:

```
[ARPANET (1969)] ──> [NSFNET (1980s)] ──> [The Internet (Modern)]
```

### A. ARPANET (1969)
* **Full Form:** Advanced Research Projects Agency Network.
* **Origin:** Developed by the U.S. Department of Defense.
* **Significance:** The first network to connect computers at universities (UCLA, Stanford, UC Santa Barbara, University of Utah) to share research files.

### B. NSFNET (1980s)
* **Full Form:** National Science Foundation Network.
* **Origin:** Created by the National Science Foundation in the United States.
* **Significance:** A high-speed network designed specifically for academic research. Unlike ARPANET, it did not allow any commercial/business traffic initially.

### C. The Internet (Modern Era)
* As private commercial networks connected to NSFNET and ARPANET, the system grew into a global "network of networks," known as the **Internet**.
* **WWW (World Wide Web):** In 1990, Tim Berners-Lee created the WWW, introducing websites, web browsers, and hyperlinks, making the Internet easy for the general public to navigate.

## 3. Classification of Networks by Geographical Scale

Networks are classified into four main categories based on the distance they cover:

```
[ PAN (<10m) ] ──> [ LAN (<1km) ] ──> [ MAN (<50km) ] ──> [ WAN (Global) ]
```

### A. PAN (Personal Area Network)
* **Range:** Very small, typically up to **10 meters**.
* **Use Case:** Connecting personal devices in close range.
* **Example:** Connecting a smartphone to wireless earbuds via **Bluetooth**, or sharing a file between two phones.

### B. LAN (Local Area Network)
* **Range:** Small geographical area, typically up to **1 km** (a room, office building, or school campus).
* **Use Case:** Private network connecting workstations and sharing local resources.
* **Example:** A school computer laboratory or office Wi-Fi network.
* **Traits:** Private ownership, very high speeds (up to 1 Gbps), and very low error rates.

### C. MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)
* **Range:** Spans a city, town, or metropolitan area, typically up to **50 km**.
* **Use Case:** Connecting multiple LANs across a city.
* **Example:** A cable TV network spanning Chennai, or a university connecting its north and south city campuses.

### D. WAN (Wide Area Network)
* **Range:** Spans a country, continent, or the entire globe.
* **Use Case:** Connecting distant offices across states or nations.
* **Example:** The global Internet, or a multinational bank's international branch network.
* **Traits:** Public/shared ownership (telecom carriers), lower speeds than LAN, and higher error rates due to distance.

---

## 4. Key Summary: Comparing LAN and WAN

In CBSE examinations, comparing LAN and WAN is a frequent question.

| Feature | LAN (Local Area Network) | WAN (Wide Area Network) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Geographical Area** | Small (up to 1 km; rooms, office buildings). | Large (spans states, countries, or globe). |
| **Speed (Data Rate)** | Very high speed (10 Mbps to 1 Gbps). | Lower speed than LAN due to massive distances. |
| **Error Rate** | Very low (runs on dedicated private wires). | High (runs on public/telecom lines). |
| **Transmission Media** | Coaxial cable, twisted pair, local Wi-Fi. | Satellites, telephone lines, fiber-optic cables. |
| **Ownership** | Private (owned by a single company/person). | Public/Shared (telecom providers). |
